What AB 2017 does, in plain language

  • Adds Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha to the state holiday list (Government Code 6700, SEC. 7). Dates follow the Islamic lunar calendar.
  • Courts stay open. Both dates are excluded from judicial holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 135, SEC. 2).
  • Schools and community colleges may close only through a memorandum of understanding with employee unions (Education Code 37220.7 and 79020, SEC. 3, SEC. 5).
  • Paid holidays for classified staff through the same agreements (Education Code 45203 and 88203, SEC. 4, SEC. 6).
  • State employees may elect eight hours of holiday credit for Eid in lieu of a personal holiday (Government Code 19853 and 19853.1, SEC. 8, SEC. 9).
  • School exercises “exploring the history” of Eid and an optional State Board of Education model curriculum guide (Education Code 37220.7(e), SEC. 3).
  • No student excused-absence guarantee. The Education Code 48205 amendment in the introduced bill was removed on June 16, 2026 (see what changed).

An act to amend Section 135 of the Code of Civil Procedure, to amend Sections 37220.7, 45203, 79020, and 88203 of the Education Code, and to amend Sections 6700, 19853, and 19853.1 of the Government Code, relating to state holidays.

Legislative Counsel’s Digest

AB 2017, as amended, Haney. State holidays: Eid.

Existing law designates specific days as holidays in this state. Existing law adopts state holidays as judicial holidays, with certain exceptions, including Admission Day and Columbus Day. Existing law designates holidays on which community colleges and public schools are authorized to close pursuant to a memorandum of understanding between the governing board and represented employees, including “Native American Day” on the 4th Friday in September.

This bill would add “Eid al-Fitr” and “Eid al-Adha” to the list of state holidays. The bill would exclude “Eid al-Fitr” and “Eid al-Adha” from designation as judicial holidays. The bill would authorize community colleges and public schools to close on “Eid al-Fitr” and “Eid al-Adha,” as specified.

Existing law entitles state employees, with specified exceptions, to be given time off with pay for specified holidays. Existing law, in addition, authorizes state employees to elect to use 8 hours of vacation, annual leave, compensating time off, or personal holiday credit corresponding with specified dates.

This bill would, similarly, permit the employee to elect to receive the holiday credit for the holidays of “Eid al-Fitr” or “Eid al-Adha,” as specified. The bill would, for those holidays whose dates vary annually, instead authorize the above-described election to be made on the date designated by their group.

The bill would make findings and declarations related to the purpose of the Eid holidays. The bill would make its provisions severable.

This bill would incorporate additional changes to Section 135 of the Code of Civil Procedure proposed by AB 2294 to be operative only if this bill and AB 2294 are enacted and this bill is enacted last. The bill also would incorporate additional changes to Sections 45203, 79020, and 88203 of the Education Code proposed by SB 1394 to be operative only if this bill and SB 1394 are enacted and this bill is enacted last. The bill further would incorporate additional changes to Section 6700 of the Government Code proposed by AB 2294 and SB 1394 to be operative only if this bill and either or both of those bills are enacted and this bill is enacted last. The bill further would incorporate additional changes to Sections 19853 and 19853.1 of the Government Code proposed by SB 1394 and AB 1841 to be operative only if this bill and either or both of those bills are enacted and this bill is enacted last.

Bill Text

The people of the State of California do enact as follows:

SECTION 1. Findings and declarations

(a) California is home to communities with diverse cultural traditions. The state has an interest in administering its public calendars, educational institutions, and workforce policies in a manner that reflects California’s population and provides equal access to public education, public employment, and civic life.

(b) Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are culturally significant and this act seeks to accommodate individuals’ abilities to celebrate these holidays.

(c) Predictable, highly concentrated spikes in student and public employee absenteeism occur annually during these periods. California law acknowledges several dates associated with cultural and historical traditions as state holidays. Inclusion in the state’s civil holiday calendar does not, by itself, require the closure of courts, state offices, public schools, community colleges, local agencies, or private businesses.

(d) The designation of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha as state holidays is a civil calendar determination that acknowledges their cultural significance to California communities. The designation does not require any person or public institution to participate in a ceremony, event, or other activity.

(e) The civil purposes of this act are to provide predictable notice of dates on which Californians may request leave or an excused absence, facilitate orderly staffing and academic planning, reduce avoidable scheduling conflicts, protect public school average daily attendance funding metrics, and administer state holiday and leave policies consistently across comparable cultural observances.

(f) Following the standard established by the U.S. Supreme Court in Groff v. DeJoy (2023) 600 U.S. 447, public employers face significantly heightened legal burdens when reviewing individual accommodation requests. This act supplements, and does not diminish, existing protections permitting pupils to receive excused absences and permitting employees to use otherwise available leave benefits for personal and cultural observances.

(g) A public school or community college may modify its calendar only through otherwise applicable local decisionmaking and collective bargaining procedures and in compliance with instructional and educational requirements. This local process allows consideration of regional attendance patterns, staffing needs, instructional continuity, fiscal effects, and community circumstances.

(h) Any civil benefit provided under this act shall be available without regard to a person’s cultural background or personal beliefs. A person shall not be required to participate in an event or demonstrate adherence to any custom or tradition.

(i) Public educational institutions may provide objective academic instruction concerning the history, cultural traditions, and civic significance of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. Nothing in this act requires a pupil or employee to participate in an instructional exercise, ceremony, or cultural activity.

(j) The dates identified pursuant to this act are civil reference dates established for predictable public administration. The designation of a civil reference date does not limit an individual’s ability to request leave or an excused absence on another date associated with that individual’s cultural practices.

(k) The Legislature intends through this act to acknowledge the cultural significance of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha while preserving local control, maintaining continuity of public services, and avoiding unnecessary fiscal, educational, and operational burdens.

(l) The Legislature intends this act to be interpreted and implemented consistently with the United States Constitution and the California Constitution.

What changed between versions

Our analysis, checked against each version on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (introduced Feb 17; amended May 18, Jun 16, Aug 13, Aug 21, Aug 27, 2026).

June 16, 2026 — the student excused-absence guarantee was removed

The introduced bill amended Education Code 48205 so that Eid observance would be a required excused absence. The digest said:

This bill would add an absence for purposes of observing “Eid al-Fitr” or “Eid al-Adha” to the list of required excused absences.

The Senate author’s amendments of June 16 deleted that section. Existing law (Education Code 48205(a)(7)) already allows excused absences for religious observance with a parent’s written request, which the final findings in Section 1(f) acknowledge.

August 13, 2026 — “celebrating” became “exploring the history”

From introduction through June 16, Education Code 37220.7(e) authorized school exercises:

acknowledging and celebrating the meaning and importance of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha

That wording was copied from the 2025 Diwali statute (AB 268, Chapter 358), which still reads that way in subdivision (d). After public criticism, the Senate Appropriations amendments of August 13 changed the Eid clause to:

exploring the history of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha

The State Board of Education’s authority to adopt a model curriculum guide was kept. See SEC. 3.

August 13–27, 2026 — findings rewritten, “religion” removed

The final Section 1 findings describe the holidays as “culturally significant” and the designation as “a civil calendar determination,” name protecting “public school average daily attendance funding metrics” as a purpose, and cite Groff v. DeJoy (2023) for the burden of individual accommodation requests. The final text does not contain the words “religion,” “religious,” or “Muslim.” See Section 1.

August 21–27, 2026 — coordination with three other holiday bills

Sections 2.5, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, 7.1–7.3, 8.1–8.3 and 9.1–9.3 are alternate versions of the same code sections that become operative only if AB 2294 (Sylvia Mendez Day), SB 1394 (Cesar Chavez Day / Farmworkers Day), or AB 1841 (Native American Day) are also enacted. Sections 11–17 explain which version applies.

Questions about the text

What is the current version of AB 2017?

The version on this page is AB 2017 as amended in the Senate on August 27, 2026. It is the text the Senate passed 30–4 and the Assembly concurred in 62–3 on August 30, 2026, and the text sent to Governor Newsom. Earlier versions: introduced February 17, 2026; amended May 18, June 16, August 13, and August 21, 2026.

Does AB 2017 guarantee excused absences for students?

Not in the final text. The introduced bill amended Education Code 48205 to add Eid to the list of required excused absences; that section was removed in the June 16, 2026 Senate amendments. Section 1(f) says the act supplements existing protections, which already allow excused absences for religious observance with a parent’s written request.

Does AB 2017 create a paid day off for everyone?

No. State employees may elect eight hours of holiday credit for Eid al-Fitr or Eid al-Adha in lieu of eight hours of personal holiday credit (SEC. 8). Classified school and community college employees may receive a paid holiday only if their governing board agrees to it in a memorandum of understanding (SEC. 4, SEC. 6). State offices and courts do not close.

What are the school exercises and curriculum guide provisions?

Education Code 37220.7(e) as amended says public schools and educational institutions may include exercises, funded through existing resources, exploring the history of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, and that the State Board of Education may adopt a model curriculum guide for those exercises (SEC. 3). From February 17 through June 16, 2026 the clause read “acknowledging and celebrating the meaning and importance” of Eid; it was changed on August 13, 2026.

When would AB 2017 take effect?

If Governor Newsom signs it by September 30, 2026, it takes effect January 1, 2027 as a regular statute. The alternate sections become operative only if AB 2294, SB 1394, or AB 1841 are also enacted, as explained in Sections 11–17.
