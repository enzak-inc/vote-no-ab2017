About AB 2017

AB 2017, introduced by Assemblymember Matt Haney (D-San Francisco) on February 17, 2026, adds Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha to California's list of state holidays. In its final form the bill lets school and community college districts close on those dates through union agreements, lets classified staff take them as paid holidays through the same agreements, lets state employees elect Eid holiday credit, and authorizes Eid exercises in public schools with a state model curriculum guide. Courts stay open.

The bill is sponsored by the Muslim Impact Council, CAIR-CA, and the California Commission on Asian and Pacific Islander American Affairs (CAPIAA). It passed the Assembly 64–1 (May 26), the Senate 30–4 (Aug 30), and a final Assembly concurrence vote 62–3 the same day. It is now being enrolled and sent to Governor Newsom, who has until September 30, 2026 to sign or veto. A veto is the only remaining way to stop it.

Concerns from opponents center on California's traditional neutrality toward specific religious holidays, equal-treatment questions across faiths, and the parts of the final text that got no coverage — see what's actually in the final text below.

What's Actually in the Final Text

Plain-language summary of AB 2017 as amended August 27, 2026. Read the official text.

Adds both Eids to the state holiday list
Government Code 6700. Dates are set by the Islamic lunar calendar: the first day of Shawwal and the 10th day of Dhu al-Hijjah.
Courts stay open
Both dates are excluded from judicial holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 135).
Schools and community colleges may close, only by union agreement
A district can close on the Eid dates if its board reaches a memorandum of understanding with employee unions (Education Code 37220.7, 79020). Same mechanism as Native American Day and Diwali.
Paid holidays for classified staff, by the same agreements
Classified school and community college employees may receive a paid holiday on both dates if the board agrees in the MOU (Education Code 45203, 88203).
State employees may elect Eid holiday credit
8 hours of holiday credit for Eid in lieu of a personal holiday (Government Code 19853, 19853.1). Not a new paid day off for everyone.
Eid exercises in schools, plus a state curriculum guide
Schools may hold exercises “exploring the history of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha,” and the State Board of Education may adopt a model curriculum guide (Education Code 37220.7(e)).
No new student excused-absence guarantee
The introduced bill added Eid to required excused absences (Education Code 48205). That section was removed on June 16. Existing law already allows religious absences with a parent’s note.

What the coverage missed

Every quote below is verbatim from the August 27, 2026 text. We oppose this bill. Read the quotes and judge for yourself. The complete bill is on our AB 2017 full text page.

The word “religion” appears zero times
Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are culturally significant … The designation of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha as state holidays is a civil calendar determination
Two religious holidays are written into law as “cultural” and a “civil calendar determination.” The bill never uses the words religion, religious, or Muslim. Earlier drafts did; the final text scrubbed them.
Its own findings name attendance money as a purpose
The civil purposes of this act are to … protect public school average daily attendance funding metrics
Districts lose state funding when students are absent. Closing school on Eid protects the funding formula. The Legislature put that in writing.
A blanket holiday instead of individual accommodation
Following the standard established by the U.S. Supreme Court in Groff v. DeJoy (2023) 600 U.S. 447, public employers face significantly heightened legal burdens when reviewing individual accommodation requests.
The findings argue that case-by-case religious accommodation is now too burdensome for public employers, so the state should designate the days instead.
Paid days for staff, decided in union negotiations
the classified service may be entitled to a paid holiday on … “Eid al-Fitr” … “Eid al-Adha” … if the governing board, pursuant to a memorandum of understanding … agrees to the paid holiday.
Education Code 45203 and 88203. Two more paid holidays become a bargaining chip in every school and community college district. Districts carry the cost.
Eid exercises in classrooms and a state model curriculum
public schools and educational institutions throughout this state may include exercises, funded through existing resources, exploring the history of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. The State Board of Education may adopt a model curriculum guide
Education Code 37220.7(e). From February through June the same clause read “acknowledging and celebrating the meaning and importance” of Eid, copied word for word from the 2025 Diwali law. It was softened on August 13 after public criticism. As the California Family Council points out, no California statute authorizes school exercises for Christmas, Easter, or Good Friday.
Bargaining units pick the date
For those holidays whose dates vary annually, the employee may instead make the election on the dates designated by their group.
Government Code 19853. Because Eid moves with the lunar calendar, the date a state employee takes off is set by their bargaining unit, not by the calendar.
Moving dates that depend on the moon
the first day of the month of Shawwal in the Islamic lunar calendar … the 10th day of the month of Dhu al-Hijjah in the Islamic lunar calendar
Neither holiday has a fixed civil date. They shift about 11 days earlier each year and can fall on different days for different communities depending on moon sighting.
The student protection sponsors advertised was deleted
This bill would add an absence for purposes of observing “Eid al-Fitr” or “Eid al-Adha” to the list of required excused absences.
That sentence is from the introduced bill (Feb 17). The Senate removed the excused-absence section on June 16. The sponsors’ fact sheet still claims the bill “guarantees excused absences for students.” What survived is paid days for staff, optional closures, and optional curriculum.
Calendar creep
operative only if this bill and AB 2294 are enacted … SB 1394 … AB 1841
AB 2017 sits on the Governor’s desk alongside three more holiday bills this month: Sylvia Mendez Day (AB 2294), Farmworkers Day (SB 1394), and Native American Day changes (AB 1841). Diwali was added in 2025, Lunar New Year in 2022.
